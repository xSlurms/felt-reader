Before you start

Felt Reader is a learning aid, not a solver. It's built to help you think through poker decisions and spot common leaks — not to hand you a guaranteed-correct answer.

What it does well: estimates your equity, reads the board for flushes, straights, and paired dangers, and flags when aggression means you're probably beat. It's good for training your instincts on when to call, fold, and give up.

What it can't know: it assumes a normal opponent and a normal betting range. Real players deviate. Against a wild bluffer or a rock, the read can be wrong. It doesn't know your position, stack depth, or history with the player — all of which matter. Treat the recommendation as a second opinion, not gospel.

It won't make you win money. Poker has variance; good decisions lose all the time in the short run. This tool can improve your decisions, but no tool controls the cards or guarantees results. Don't wager more than you can afford to lose.

How the numbers are made. Equity is a Monte Carlo simulation — 14,000 random run-outs per read, so the percentage wobbles by about 1% between taps. On the flop it counts one street only: you see the turn, then decide again. Before the flop the verdict comes from the starting-hand chart, not the pot odds, because calling a raise buys three more streets rather than a showdown.

Educational use only. Not financial or gambling advice. You are responsible for your own play.

▸ Headspace — the part the math can't do tap to expand
Tilt is the one thing that's actually yours. The cards aren't. The coolers and suckouts aren't. Whether you keep playing while frustrated is. The moment you notice it, that's the signal to stop — not to prove something.
Don't fight fire with fire. A player betting every street is betting weak hands too. Don't try to out-bluff them — you can't bluff someone who never folds. Tighten up, cut your bluffs to zero, and let them bet into your good hands.
Charge draws, don't fear them. Ahead on a wet board? Bet big — not to fold them out, but to make chasing expensive. When they call badly and hit, they still called badly. The answer to getting drawn out on is to charge more, never less.
Read the story, not just your cards. A caller who suddenly bombs the river the moment the flush completes has the flush. What they did across the whole hand says more than what you're holding.
Fear and stubbornness cost the same. Calling down with weak pairs bleeds money. So does folding top pair because a big bet scared you. Both are leaks — know which one you're doing today.
The big hands lose the big pots. Aces on T-9-8 is one pair. A flush that isn't the nut flush is second best. Strong-looking hands are exactly where stacks disappear.
Table talk is noise. Players lie, misremember, or tell the truth at random. Your decision was right or wrong based on what you knew when you made it — not on what you found out after.
Never chase it back. Playing bigger or looser to get even is how a roll dies. Decide what you're willing to lose before you sit down, and let that be the number.
When you can't tell whether you're misplaying a spot or just rattled by it: if it's the spot, work the adjustment. If it's you, leave. No adjustment works while you're tilted.

3 — The action
Mark what happened on each street: they bet into you (with size), I bet and they called (your size), or checked. Bets they fire and streets they call both narrow their range. The current street sets the price you're facing.
Every read below assumes one opponent. With more than one player in the hand, tighten up — someone connects with the board far more often, so marginal calls become folds and thin value bets stop being worth it. After the flop it doesn't know your position. Your seat drives the preflop read only — whether it's folded to you, someone limped, someone raised, or you raised and got played back at. Once there's a board, lean to the tighter side of anything marginal when you're out of position, and use the Which hands to open guide above.
Tap your hand on the chart to see a read. Pick your seat and mark the Preflop row for a before-the-flop verdict, or add a board and a bet size for a postflop one.
▸ What the bet sizes mean tap to expand
Compare their bet to the pot before they bet — most sites show the pot and the bet separately, so it's the two numbers already on your screen. If you're between two, pick the bigger one.
Break-even is how often you need to win for calling to be worth it. Beat that number and the call makes money; miss it and it doesn't.
The Preflop row is labelled differently — Min 2x, 2.5x, 3x, 4x, 5x+ — because before the flop money goes in as raises, and a raise is measured against the blind, not against the pot. Pick the multiple closest to what they made it.
▸ Draw cheat sheet (call/fold in your head) tap to expand
Facing a bet with a draw and no made hand? Compare their bet to the pot. Call if it's at or under the max; above it, fold unless stacks are deep enough to win big when you hit.

Overcards are dirty outs. Hitting top pair doesn't always win. Don't chase them alone — discount to about half their outs.
Flush "need 1" (4 to a flush on board) is the same price but a warning, not a green light — others are on it too, and your flush may be beaten.
Bigger than a third of the pot? The pot alone isn't paying you. Continue only with implied odds (deep stacks, you win a big bet when you hit) and a strong, disguised draw. Shallow stacks or an obvious draw → fold.
These are one-card prices (you pay per street). Seeing both turn and river roughly doubles the odds — but only if it's checked through or you're all-in.
▸ Short-stack push/fold (SNGs) tap to expand
When your stack is short (~15 big blinds or less) in a sit'n'go, you shove all-in or fold — no small raises. Pick your stack, your seat, and whether you're first in or facing a shove. Uses your hole cards from above.
What counts as short? It's your stack vs the blinds, not vs other players. 20+ bb = play normal poker (use the tool above). 15 bb = top of push/fold range. 10 bb or less = pure shove-or-fold. SNGs start deep (~50 bb), so this only matters mid-to-late once blinds climb.
